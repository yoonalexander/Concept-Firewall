# Design review

Reviewed October 20, 2026. Scope: the complete supplied `Concept_Firewall_Design_Document.md`, the initial repository state, and the primary sources listed below. This review makes no claim that model inference or a project experiment has succeeded.

## Assessment

The proposed staged research project is sound. It correctly separates intermediate correctness from eventual correctness, prediction from recoverability, and observational analysis from live intervention. Puzzle-grouped evaluation, strict clue-preserving Sudoku validation, source pinning, and explicit negative results are appropriate foundations. The design also correctly limits Sudoku findings to structural reliability.

Implementation should begin with the local verifier, versioned records, provenance, environment checks, and upstream-equivalent baseline. Advance to observational trajectories only after real baseline inference succeeds, then to probes only after final-output equivalence succeeds. A tested local foundation is useful progress when hardware or dependencies prevent the inference gate from passing; it is not a completed baseline reproduction.

## Findings and decisions

**Finding confirmed during implementation:** the proposed direct decoding of each `z_levels[k]` does not produce full candidate boards at this upstream revision. Those tensors have each level's shorter sequence length, and `z_hats` aliases the final accumulator. The implementation now snapshots the full cumulative prefix through upstream's accumulation API, with no upstream patch. Actual-source regression tests cover full length, final equivalence and future independence. Initial native-length data/probe reports are explicitly superseded in `results/reports/reconstruction_correction.md`.

| Priority | Finding | Decision / acceptance condition |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | A single deterministic trajectory per puzzle does not identify within-puzzle trajectory risk separately from puzzle difficulty. Frozen intermediate states are functions of the input under this policy. Better latent AUROC than clue count alone does not demonstrate a trajectory-specific mechanism. | Describe initial results as prediction across unseen puzzles under a fixed generator. Include the raw clue grid or a comparably capable input representation in difficulty controls, alongside cheap handcrafted features. Reserve conditional completion-risk claims for separately validated stochastic continuations. |
| P1 | Reconstructing every level after `generate()` returns supports observational features but cannot measure when each generator level completed. Dividing total runtime among levels or timing post-hoc decoding would give misleading lead times. | Record total generation and reconstruction costs separately. Keep incremental generator timings unavailable until actual generation-boundary instrumentation measures them. Never infer speedups from the 12-level count. |
| P1 | The 32-input smoke run may have too few failures to train, calibrate, or evaluate a classifier. AUROC is undefined for a split containing only one outcome class. | Treat smoke output as operational evidence only. Report unique puzzle counts and success/failure counts; return unavailable metrics for degenerate labels. Predetermine a larger sampling plan and uncertainty requirements before evaluating probes. Do not manufacture balanced test prevalence by discarding outcomes. |
| P1 | The source document leaves threshold selection, calibration reuse, and selection across many levels/features partly discretionary. Choosing the best reported level on test data would bias the result. | Freeze puzzle manifests before fitting. Use training-only transformations, validation-only hyperparameter/level selection, and a separate calibration split when feasible. Predeclare a primary comparison or adjust/report the search over levels and features. Fit calibration on unweighted held-out data reflecting evaluation prevalence, especially after class-weighted training. |
| P2 | A level-only classifier has no signal in a complete dataset where every trajectory contributes every level: each level has the same final-failure prevalence. Apparent discrimination may indicate missingness or leakage. | Keep this as a constant/prevalence sanity control. Diagnose level-dependent missing records before treating an effect as meaningful. Report exclusions and capture failures rather than silently dropping trajectories. |
| P2 | A threshold with 5% false-positive rate at one level does not guarantee 5% probability of any false alarm over repeated monitoring. | For later online policies, select and evaluate the complete sequential trigger on held-out trajectories. Distinguish per-level FPR from trajectory-level probability of any false alarm; include monitoring cost at every invocation. |
| P2 | Numeric codebook indices are categorical labels; their arbitrary numbering supplies no meaningful distance or arithmetic ordering. | Prefer categorical histograms or frozen codebook embeddings. Do not interpret the arithmetic mean, standard deviation, or norm of raw IDs as concept geometry. Limit dimensionality and fit any learned projection on training puzzles only. |
| P2 | Final correctness, outcome metadata, future levels, and feature records are close together in the proposed schema. An accidental merge can leak the target into features. Puzzle IDs also require a stable definition. | Store outcomes separately from decision-time state, whitelist feature columns, and test that perturbing future records cannot change earlier features. Derive puzzle identity from a canonical clue grid, retain dataset row provenance, group exact duplicates together, and document treatment of Sudoku symmetry-related puzzles. |
| P2 | “Verified upstream integration points” in a proposal are source assertions, not an executable compatibility guarantee. A matching last decode alone does not validate every intermediate prefix. | Pin the actual upstream commit and artifact revisions/hashes. Confirm CLI, tokenizer formatting, BOS/EOS handling, cumulative-prefix reconstruction, and generation arguments from source. Test final equivalence with real checkpoints; label fake-adapter unit tests as unit tests. Keep upstream patches isolated and documented. |

## Scope and release gates

1. **Local foundation:** strict parsing/verification, explicit schema validation, puzzle identity, manifests, environment inspection, upstream compatibility checks, and tests. These can run without downloaded weights.
2. **Baseline reproduction (M0):** official checkpoint and selected data load; official deterministic inference actually runs; inputs, raw outputs, exact command, upstream SHA, artifact hashes, environment, and deviations are recorded. An invocation that only launches an unsuccessfully completed subprocess does not pass.
3. **Observation/labels (M1–M2):** record all expected levels from real runs, enforce prefix-only features, preserve malformed outputs as failures, and demonstrate the final reconstruction matches baseline. Post-hoc traces remain full-depth observations.
4. **Probes (M3):** adequate outcome diversity, frozen puzzle splits, simple input/decoded controls, training-only preprocessing, calibration discipline, held-out paired comparisons, and puzzle-level uncertainty. Report null findings.
5. **Deferred:** transformer hooks, resumed stochastic suffixes, intervention, natural-language extensions, and a viewer retain the design's evidence gates. No output from an offline synthetic fixture is evidence that these gates passed.

The user's request is to review and implement the project. The document supplies proposed requirements and a roadmap; its embedded work order is not an independent authorization to publish results, purchase compute, enable paid services, expose a hosted endpoint, or contact others. Preserve the original design as supplied and record implementation deviations explicitly.

## Evidence boundaries and source checks

- The [NCM paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14162) verifies the architecture's published two-stage, coarse-to-fine formulation. Its reported benchmark results belong to the upstream authors, not this project.
- The [official evaluator](https://github.com/HazyResearch/ncm/blob/main/evaluate.py), [shard runner](https://github.com/HazyResearch/ncm/blob/main/scripts/eval_shard.py), and [hard-Sudoku configuration](https://github.com/HazyResearch/ncm/blob/main/config/eval/sudoku-hard.yaml) are integration sources. These mutable links are pointers; reproducibility requires the exact local pinned source and hashes recorded by each run.
- The [ACL primary paper](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-srw.15.pdf) and [authors' code](https://github.com/guan404ming/dlm-probing) confirm related probing work exists. The paper also discusses prompt difficulty, reinforcing the need for strong input controls. Its empirical claims are not replicated here.
- The [ICLR A2D proceedings entry](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/956e5427549a82a7472e02adc88360e9-Abstract-Conference.html) confirms a separate diffusion-model safety-alignment method; it does not establish NCM safety performance.
- The [medical-probe publisher record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S153204642600105X) was verified through the publisher's indexed abstract; direct full-page access returned HTTP 403. No full-text methodological audit or clinical-performance verification was performed.

This was a focused design/source review, not an exhaustive novelty search. All project-specific accuracy, calibration, recoverability, intervention benefit, latency, and safety claims remain unverified until their corresponding recorded experiments run.
